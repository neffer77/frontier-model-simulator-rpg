# Phase 4D.13 — Governance Conflicts, Board Votes & Founder-Control Risk

Phase 4D.13 turns the cap table, financing covenants, and board composition into an actual control system. The player can no longer assume every strategic decision is theirs to make.

## Board votes

The board can vote on emergency financing, roadmap acceleration, reliability pauses, strategic sale processes, and CEO confidence reviews. Votes are generated from the current board composition and each constituency's incentives.

Founder, investor, and strategic directors have different default preferences. Runway, reliability confidence, board confidence, and crisis conditions alter their support.

## Investor vetoes

Some financing and sale motions are veto-sensitive. If prior financing created meaningful covenant pressure, an investor "no" vote can block the motion even when the overall board would otherwise approve it.

Vetoes reduce founder authority and can weaken board confidence.

## Founder-control risk

The Governance dashboard calculates founder-control risk from three persistent sources:

- founder equity ownership
- founder share of board seats
- accumulated founder authority

A founder may therefore remain economically significant while losing practical control through board composition and governance concessions.

## CEO confidence reviews

The board can open a CEO confidence review. The outcome considers board confidence, delivery credibility, and runway.

Strong crisis performance can result in the founder being retained with greater authority. Poor performance can remove the founder from the CEO role and install a board-appointed executive while preserving the founder's remaining economic ownership.

## Fiduciary posture

The player can explicitly prioritize survival/runway, frontier mission, or balanced stakeholders. These choices shift investor patience, product confidence, board confidence, and founder authority rather than producing a single universally correct answer.

## Integration

Phase 4D.13 builds directly on the financing and distressed-restructuring systems. Board seats added by financing rounds now have operational meaning, covenant-heavy term sheets can create veto rights, acquisition processes can require governance approval, and deteriorating runway increases leadership risk.

Browser/mobile and Scriptable WebView builds both load the governance module.
