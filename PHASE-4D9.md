# Phase 4D.9 — Roadmap Commitments, Enterprise SLAs & Executive Pressure

Phase 4D.9 connects reliability engineering to commercial and executive consequences. The player is no longer optimizing a model company with unlimited schedule flexibility: customers, sales, product, executives, and the board now expect commitments.

## Roadmap commitments

Players can commit to flagship model launches, enterprise capability deliveries, and training-platform milestones. Each promise has a due day, opportunity value, affected infrastructure components, and delivery pressure.

Commitments become riskier when their dependent components have stressed error budgets or when the release gate is frozen. Players can deliver, renegotiate the date, or accept executive acceleration that shortens the schedule while consuming reliability headroom.

Late commitments reduce executive credibility, customer trust, and eventually board confidence. Successful delivery improves credibility and converts the opportunity into modeled revenue.

## Enterprise SLAs

Players can sign strategic, regulated, and growth enterprise contracts with different availability targets, account counts, ARR, and service-credit severity.

Operational incidents are evaluated against the active SLA portfolio. Severe or long incidents can create contract breaches, service-credit penalties, customer-trust loss, and revenue impact. Stricter regulated contracts create more commercial upside but much less tolerance for reliability failures.

## Executive pressure

Executive pressure rises as commitments and contracts accumulate or deadlines are missed. The player can respond by renegotiating commitments or accepting acceleration.

Acceleration is intentionally not free: it moves the deadline closer, increases executive pressure, reduces reliability stakeholder confidence, and consumes error-budget headroom on the systems needed for the commitment.

## Stakeholder confidence

The executive board tracks confidence across sales, product, reliability, and customers. Decisions improve one constituency while potentially harming another, creating a real organizational tradeoff rather than a single optimal meter.

## Integration

Phase 4D.9 builds directly on Phase 4D.8 release governance. Reliability release freezes can block roadmap delivery, operational incidents can breach enterprise SLAs, and customer/revenue state remains shared with the release-governance system.

Browser/mobile and Scriptable WebView builds both load the new module.
