# Phase 4D.29 — Insurance, Indemnification, D&O Exposure, Reserves, Audit Committees, Internal Controls & Enterprise Risk Management

Phase 4D.29 adds a formal enterprise-risk layer above the legal, policy, communications, and governance systems.

## Insurance and risk transfer

The company now tracks D&O, cyber, and professional-liability coverage. Additional limits cost cash, but reduce the portion of modeled legal and crisis exposure that remains on the company balance sheet.

Coverage is not free protection: premiums consume capital that could otherwise fund products, compute, or hiring.

## Reserves

The player can set aside explicit legal and incident reserves. Reserves reduce uninsured exposure, but move cash out of immediately deployable operating capital.

## D&O exposure

Director-and-officer exposure rises with unresolved legal matters, weak board confidence, communications crises, and weak internal controls. Stronger control maturity reduces that exposure.

## Internal controls

Players can invest in control maturity. Stronger controls lower enterprise risk but create a small execution-speed cost, representing review, approval, documentation, and evidence requirements.

## Audit committee and findings

The player can run internal audits. Audits may surface control findings of varying severity. Findings remain open until remediated and cost money to close.

Remediation improves control maturity and audit-committee effectiveness. Ignoring findings leaves the company with weaker governance resilience.

## Risk appetite

The board can operate under conservative, balanced, or aggressive risk appetite.

Conservative appetite improves controls but slows execution. Aggressive appetite creates modest execution upside while increasing D&O exposure. Balanced appetite avoids either extreme.

## Enterprise risk score

The ERM score combines uninsured exposure, D&O exposure, control weakness, and audit-committee effectiveness. It therefore summarizes whether the company is retaining more risk than its governance systems can realistically absorb.

Enterprise risk feeds back into board confidence and interacts with the legal, regulatory, communications, and execution systems from earlier Phase 4D slices.
