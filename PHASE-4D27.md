# Phase 4D.27 — Public Communications, Media Cycles, Reputation Crises, Transparency Reports, Leaks & Executive Testimony

Phase 4D.27 makes public reputation a persistent operating system rather than a one-off event modifier.

## Media cycles

The company can face safety narratives, leaked internal memos, benchmark controversies, employee-culture stories, and policy backlash. Each media cycle creates crisis pressure and affects media sentiment and spokesperson credibility.

## Crisis responses

Active narratives can be handled with a transparent response, a public defense, or silence. Transparency is more expensive but improves public trust and disclosure credibility. Defensive messaging can stabilize sentiment and spokesperson confidence. Silence costs less but risks gradual trust erosion.

## Reputation dynamics

Media sentiment, public trust, spokesperson credibility, transparency, and crisis pressure persist across days. Higher trust and credibility improve recovery from negative cycles, while accumulated crisis pressure makes reputation harder to stabilize.

## Transparency reports

The company can publish transparency reports at a direct operating cost. Reports improve transparency, public trust, and spokesperson credibility while creating a durable record of disclosure behavior.

## Executive testimony

Players can prepare executives for testimony and then send them into a public hearing. Performance depends on spokesperson credibility, government trust, and existing regulatory risk.

Strong testimony improves public sentiment and government trust. Poor testimony can worsen both.

## Cross-system effects

Public trust now feeds customer trust, media sentiment affects recruiting reputation, and public credibility influences government relationships. Communications decisions therefore affect enterprise growth, talent, regulatory posture, and long-term company value.
