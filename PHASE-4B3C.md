# Phase 4B.3C — Careers + Mentoring

This phase turns the NPC roster into a persistent engineering organization with careers, skill growth, mentoring, workload pressure, and promotion evidence.

## Included

- Domain-specific employee experience derived from real incident participation, corrective actions, and knowledge contributions.
- Leadership skills including technical judgment, incident leadership, communication, mentoring, and systems thinking.
- Two junior employees for the mentoring loop: Jordan Kim and Sofia Alvarez.
- Persistent mentor/mentee relationships and mentoring sessions.
- Mentoring improves relevant domain experience for the mentee and mentoring/leadership experience for the mentor.
- Promotion reviews based on evidence rather than generic XP.
- Promotion ladder from Engineer I through Distinguished for the first implementation.
- Stretch assignments when an employee is not yet promotion-ready.
- Functional workload states: healthy, busy, overloaded, and critical.
- Workload derives from existing workload plus open corrective-action ownership and mentoring commitments.
- Relationship state expands to autonomy and psychological safety while preserving trust, respect, and alignment.
- Career events for overload, promotion readiness, and autonomy requests.
- Career decisions can redistribute work, grant ownership, or promote employees.
- Dedicated Careers UI with employee experience, leadership profile, relationships, promotion evidence, and mentoring controls.
- Responsive browser/mobile layout and Scriptable WebView integration.

## Design principles

1. Employees grow from relevant work, not universal XP.
2. Promotion is based on demonstrated scope: depth, cross-domain exposure, incident leadership, mentoring, and organizational contribution.
3. Workload is a coordination constraint, not a punishment meter.
4. Disagreeing with an employee should not inherently damage relationships; how technical decisions are made matters more than agreement.
5. Mentoring should create organizational capability rather than only improve relationship scores.

## Next organizational slice

Phase 4B.4 should add hiring and org design: candidate pipelines, interviews, compensation, headcount budgets, role fit, managers/teams, external recruiting competition, attrition, and a real org chart.

After 4B.4, Phase 4C moves into the major technical-realism expansion: editable training configs, filesystem/terminal workflows, PyTorch exercises, TensorBoard-style metrics, GPU fleet visualization, NCCL traces, and data-pipeline inspection.
