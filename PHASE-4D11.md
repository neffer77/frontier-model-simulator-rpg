# Phase 4D.11 — Financing, Strategic Transactions & Runway Crises

Phase 4D.11 turns capital strategy into a persistent gameplay system. Cash can now be raised, borrowed, or strategically exchanged, but every option changes ownership, board control, debt service, infrastructure lock-in, or future flexibility.

## Financing

Players can close an equity round, take venture debt, or accept an emergency down round. Financing terms worsen as runway and investor patience deteriorate.

Equity rounds increase cash but dilute founders/employees and can add investor board seats. Venture debt avoids immediate dilution but creates debt principal and recurring monthly service. Emergency down rounds provide crisis liquidity at materially worse dilution and valuation consequences.

## Runway crises

The capital dashboard calculates runway after debt-service pressure. Falling below three months opens a runway crisis that reduces investor patience and board confidence. Recovering above six months clears the crisis state.

## Strategic transactions

Players can sign long-term GPU supply agreements, cloud partnerships, systems-team acqui-hires, and enterprise distribution partnerships. These deals trade cash for compute capacity, hiring leverage, customer/revenue growth, or operational lock-in.

## Capital structure and board control

Founder, employee, and investor ownership are tracked as a cap table. Board seats are also tracked by founder, investor, and strategic representation. The player can negotiate additional board representation to stabilize investor patience or improve board confidence, but governance control becomes a persistent consequence of financing choices.

## Integration

Phase 4D.11 builds on Phase 4D.10 quarterly planning. Runway, valuation, investor patience, compute capacity, hiring envelopes, revenue, customer trust, roadmap credibility, and executive pressure all feed into financing terms or transaction outcomes.

Browser/mobile and Scriptable WebView builds both load the new capital-strategy module.
