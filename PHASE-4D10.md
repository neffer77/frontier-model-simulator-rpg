# Phase 4D.10 — Quarterly Planning, Board/Investor Simulation & Competitive Pressure

Phase 4D.10 turns the frontier-model company into a quarterly operating system. The player must allocate limited capital, publish forecasts, react to competitors, and defend results to the board.

## Quarterly capital allocation

Players choose a quarterly operating posture: balanced execution, frontier capability race, durable enterprise, or commercial growth. Each posture proposes a budget split across hiring, compute, reliability, and go-to-market work.

The player can edit allocations before funding the plan. Funding consumes cash and creates persistent effects: compute investment expands modeled capacity, reliability restores error-budget headroom and trust, hiring creates a future hiring envelope, and go-to-market spending lifts modeled revenue and sales confidence.

## Forecasts and forecast accuracy

The board expects explicit quarter-end forecasts for revenue, cash, and customer trust. Forecast accuracy is scored against actual game state and becomes a material part of the next board review.

This rewards calibrated planning instead of simply maximizing every metric.

## Competitive pressure

Competitor events model capability launches, pricing attacks, long-context announcements, and compute-scale advantages. Each event increases some combination of executive pressure, customer pressure, or investor impatience.

Players can accelerate the roadmap, differentiate the product strategy, or hold course. Every response has different reliability, product, and investor consequences.

## Board and investor simulation

Board reviews score the company on:

- forecast accuracy
- cash runway
- customer trust
- delivery credibility
- reliability stakeholder confidence
- missed roadmap commitments

The resulting score changes board confidence, investor patience, and modeled company valuation.

## Runway and valuation

Runway is computed from cash and modeled operating burn. Valuation responds to board-review performance and unresolved competitive threats, so aggressive spending can improve capability while simultaneously creating financing pressure.

## Integration

Phase 4D.10 directly consumes the systems introduced in 4D.8 and 4D.9: customer economics, release governance, roadmap commitments, executive credibility, reliability health, enterprise contracts, and stakeholder confidence.
